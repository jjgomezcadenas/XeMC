\author{LXeMC Reference Manual}

\begin{abstract}
This note documents the design and implementation of LXeMC, a Monte Carlo
simulation of $\gamma$ and lepton transport in liquid xenon, written to support
neutrinoless double-beta-decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) studies in an LZ-like detector
geometry. The code is organized around a single design principle: physics
fidelity should follow the analysis. Inside the fiducial volume (FV) the full
photon/lepton cascade is tracked; inside dense radioactive sources only photon
propagation is needed to compute the surface flux; everywhere else most $\gamma$
histories are vetoed before they could contaminate the FV, so a fast kernel with
two analysis-driven cuts terminates them early. The result is a staged
simulation that runs in the $10^{6}$~events/s regime while preserving the
detailed physics that drives single-site versus multi-site discrimination in the
ROI.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LXeMC simulates $\gamma$ and lepton transport in a large liquid-xenon time
projection chamber (LXe TPC), using the LZ detector as the concrete reference
configuration. The intended use cases are:

\begin{itemize}
\item background and signal rate estimation for $0\nu\beta\beta$ at the $^{136}$Xe
$Q$-value $Q_{\beta\beta} = 2.458$\,MeV;
\item fiducial-volume optimization;
\item single-site (SS) versus multi-site (MS) discrimination studies;
\item characterization of cryostat and PMT-borne backgrounds.
\end{itemize}

Detector response --- light/charge yield, PMT digitization, S1/S2 reconstruction,
trigger --- is downstream of LXeMC. The simulation writes per-deposit truth
information to disk, and a separate post-processing layer is responsible for
detector-response modeling. This decoupling is intentional: the response model
will evolve faster than the transport, and we want to refit response parameters
without regenerating the Monte Carlo.

\subsection{Design constraints}
\label{sec:constraints}

The design is shaped by four constraints:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Speed is dominant.} The simulation must run at the level of
$10^6$ events per second on representative configurations.
\item \textbf{Most events are rejected early.} For many source configurations,
gammas are vetoed in active xenon outside the FV, absorbed in passive material,
or degraded to energies below the region of interest.
\item \textbf{Detailed transport is only needed locally.} Full
photon/electron cascades and cluster structure matter primarily inside the FV.
\item \textbf{The detector partition is physically meaningful.} The geometry
reflects detector regions that carry distinct transport or classification
meaning; it is not a generic hierarchy traversed at runtime.
\end{enumerate}

The immediate consequence is that any cost spent on fully tracking obviously
rejected events is wasted. The dominant cost must be spent only on the small
subset of trajectories that survive into the FV. This leads to a staged
architecture organized around three accuracy tiers.

\subsection{Three accuracy tiers}
\label{sec:tiers}

The architecture of LXeMC is shaped by three accuracy requirements that apply
in three different parts of the geometry, with three different cost profiles.
This is the most important organizing principle in the code, and Sections
\ref{sec:fullstack}--\ref{sec:fastkernel} should be read as the implementation
of these tiers.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Tier \arabic*.}, leftmargin=2.0cm]

\item \textbf{Inside the FV: full physics.}
The deposit topology in the FV drives the SS/MS classification, which is the
principal handle on $0\nu\beta\beta$ versus $\gamma$ background discrimination.
Inside the FV the full photon/lepton cascade is tracked: Compton scattering
(Klein--Nishina, from XCOM tables), pair production (Bethe--Heitler, from XCOM),
photoelectric absorption with a K-shell vacancy deposited locally, condensed-history
electron transport with collisional $dE/dx$ from ESTAR, discrete bremsstrahlung
sampled from a Bethe--Heitler--Tsai differential cross section with explicit
Davies--Bethe--Maximon Coulomb correction, and positron annihilation into two
back-to-back 511\,keV $\gamma$s. The implemented process list (Section
\ref{sec:physics}) is complete enough for LXe at the few-MeV scale relevant to
$0\nu\beta\beta$. It is \emph{not} complete enough for high-pressure gaseous Xe
(GXe), where coherent scattering, atomic relaxation cascades, and full
multiple-scattering treatment for the $\gamma\to e^-\to$~track topology become
important; extending the physics list is on the roadmap for any future GXe
configuration.

\item \textbf{Inside dense source regions: photon-only transport.}
The radioactive source volumes (cryostat steel, MLI, PMT bodies, field-cage
structures) are simulated as photon-only material: $\gamma$s are propagated by
Klein--Nishina scattering until they exit the source or are absorbed, and any
charged secondary is taken to deposit its energy locally where it was created
(kerma approximation). This is justified because the quantity of interest
emerging from a source is the $\gamma$ flux escaping its outer surface, and the
ranges of MeV-scale electrons in titanium, PTFE, or copper are millimetre-scale,
short compared with the cm-to-decimetre dimensions of the source volumes. The
source-side transport produces a tabulated $(E, u)$ probability distribution,
where $u = \cos\theta$ to the inward surface normal (Section
\ref{sec:source-pipeline}); it is the input to the detector-side transport, not
part of it.

\item \textbf{Elsewhere in the detector: fast vetoed transport.}
Most $\gamma$ histories that originate outside the FV are vetoed before they
could contaminate it: a typical few-MeV $\gamma$ from the cryostat that passes
through the active LXe outside the FV will deposit far more than the veto
threshold along the way. Tracking such histories with the full stack is wasted
work. Outside the FV we therefore use a fast kernel
(\texttt{transport\_gamma\_fastkernel}) that combines a physics approximation ---
photon-only transport with secondaries deposited locally, like the
source-region kerma approximation --- with two analysis-driven veto criteria
(see below). Histories that survive both cuts are escalated back to the
full-physics stack at the FV boundary; histories that fail are terminated with
a recorded status code (Section \ref{sec:fastkernel}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The two cuts that define ``fast vetoed''}
\label{sec:two-cuts}

The fast kernel is governed by two visible-energy thresholds, both stored in
\texttt{sim\_config.json} so they can be tuned without recompiling:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{veto\_skin}] LZ-specific. The LZ skin is the LXe layer outside
the field cage that acts as an outer-detector veto for events leaking out of
the TPC active region. The current value is $100$\,keV, set by the LZ outer-detector
analysis. Any deposit in a Skin region above this threshold during fast-kernel
transport vetoes the event. This cut is geometry-specific: a different
detector with a different outer veto would set its own threshold and possibly
its own region tag.

\item[\texttt{veto\_TPC}] Detector-wide visible-energy floor. Any deposit in
the active TPC outside the FV (\texttt{TopActive}, \texttt{BarrelActive},
\texttt{BottomActive}) above this threshold vetoes the event. The current
value is $10$\,keV. This is \emph{not} the analysis floor used by dark-matter
searches such as XENONnT or LZ, which set $\sim$1\,keV thresholds tuned to
single-electron noise; for $0\nu\beta\beta$ the relevant signal at $Q_{\beta\beta}
= 2.458$\,MeV has a much wider resolution-broadened tail, and a 1\,keV veto
floor would risk rejecting genuine signal events whose tail leaks into the
active TPC outside the FV. XENONnT quotes $\sim 5$\,keV as a safe floor for
$0\nu\beta\beta$-style analyses; LXeMC therefore treats \texttt{veto\_TPC} as a
tunable parameter with a recommended range of $5$--$10$\,keV. The default is
the conservative end of the range.
\end{description}

A third parameter, \texttt{E\_roi\_floor} ($2.3$\,MeV by default), is a
fast-kernel optimization rather than a veto: once a $\gamma$ degrades below
this energy by Compton scattering, it can no longer contribute to the ROI
window around $Q_{\beta\beta}$, and the fast kernel terminates it locally.
This is an analysis-motivated approximation discussed in Section
\ref{sec:fastkernel}.

\subsection{What lives downstream of LXeMC}
\label{sec:downstream}

The simulation deliberately stops at the per-deposit truth level. Three things
are downstream and not part of this document:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Detector response: light/charge yield, electron drift, PMT
digitization, S1/S2 reconstruction.
\item Energy-resolution smearing and ROI window cuts (currently applied
in Python, Section \ref{sec:source-pipeline}).
\item SS/MS classification beyond the simple $z$-clustering offered in
\texttt{analysis.jl}; a more elaborate clustering or pattern-recognition layer
will likely sit between the deposit stack and the final classifier.
\end{enumerate}

The handoff format is a flat list of
\texttt{Deposit(position, energy, source, interaction, volume)} records per
accepted event (Section \ref{sec:event}); whatever the downstream pipeline
does with that list is its own concern.

\section{Physics}
\label{sec:physics}

LXeMC implements one $\gamma$ process list and one charged-particle process
list, with all energy-dependent quantities driven by tabulated NIST data.
This section is a complete inventory of \emph{what is in}; Section
\ref{sec:limitations} enumerates what is deliberately left out.

\subsection{Photon processes}
\label{sec:physics-photons}

For each step the photon transport (\texttt{transport\_photon!} in the FV stack
and \texttt{transport\_gamma\_fastkernel} elsewhere) requests three cross
sections from the material in a single call,
\begin{equation*}
(\sigma_C,\ \sigma_P,\ \sigma_{\mathrm{Ph}}) \;=\; \texttt{sigma\_three(mat, E)},
\end{equation*}
returning per-atom cross sections [cm$^2$/atom] for incoherent (Compton), pair
production (nuclear $+$ electron), and photoelectric absorption. The interaction
distance is sampled exponentially from the total macroscopic cross section
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}} = n_{\mathrm{atom}}\,(\sigma_C + \sigma_P + \sigma_{\mathrm{Ph}})$,
and the channel is selected by branching ratios.

\paragraph{Compton scattering.}
The total cross section is the NIST XCOM \emph{incoherent} channel
($Z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{KN}}$ tabulated per atom, with binding effects folded in).
Kinematics are sampled from the Klein--Nishina differential cross section by
the standard mixed inverse-CDF/rejection algorithm in
\texttt{sample\_compton}; the recoil electron is added to the particle stack
and the scattered $\gamma$ continues with the reduced energy and the rotated
direction.

\paragraph{Pair production.}
The total cross section is the sum of the XCOM \texttt{pair\_nuclear} and
\texttt{pair\_electron} channels (Bethe--Heitler with full screening).
The threshold is $2 m_e c^2 = 1.022$\,MeV; below it the channel is closed.
The energy fraction of the positron $\epsilon = E_{\mathrm{pos}}/E_\gamma$ is
sampled from the Bethe--Heitler differential cross section in
\texttt{sample\_pair}; both leptons are pushed onto the particle stack with
their kinetic energies $T = \epsilon E_\gamma - m_e c^2$ and the parent $\gamma$
is killed. Positron annihilation at rest produces two back-to-back 511\,keV
photons, generated at the end of \texttt{transport\_lepton!} when the parent
particle was a positron.

\paragraph{Photoelectric absorption.}
The total cross section is the XCOM \texttt{photoelectric} channel, summed
over all atomic shells, and includes the K-edge discontinuity at the
material's $E_K$ (for xenon, $E_K = 34.561$\,keV). On absorption, the K-shell
binding energy is deposited locally as a \texttt{:photoelectric} deposit, and
the photoelectron is created with kinetic energy $T_e = E_\gamma - E_K$. If
$T_e$ exceeds the lepton tracking cut \texttt{Te\_cut}, it is pushed onto the
stack; otherwise it is deposited locally. Below the K-edge the absorbed
$\gamma$ deposits its full energy locally with no electron emission. Atomic
relaxation (fluorescence and Auger cascades following the K-vacancy) is
\emph{not} simulated; the K-edge energy is treated as locally deposited.

\subsection{Charged-particle processes}
\label{sec:physics-leptons}

Electrons and positrons are propagated by condensed-history stepping
(\texttt{transport\_lepton!}). At each step of fixed length \texttt{ds\_step}
($0.10$\,cm by default), three things happen:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Continuous collisional energy loss.} The collisional mass
stopping power $S_{\mathrm{col}}(T)$ is interpolated from NIST ESTAR
(\texttt{dEdx\_collision}) and the energy lost over the step
$\Delta E = (S_{\mathrm{col}}/\rho)\,\rho\,\Delta s$ is deposited at the step
midpoint with the source label \texttt{:electron} or \texttt{:positron}. The
step is shortened (to $0.9\,T/(dE/dx)$) if the nominal step would deposit more
energy than the particle has.
\item \textbf{Boundary check.} The particle is advanced by \texttt{ds\_step}
and tested against the volume boundary; if it exits, transport terminates with
no further deposit on this step.
\item \textbf{Discrete bremsstrahlung.} A per-step probability
$P_{\mathrm{brems}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{atom}}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{brems}}(T)\,\Delta s,\,0.5)$
is computed from the pre-tabulated total $\sigma_{\mathrm{brems}}(T)$ for
$k > k_{\min}$ ($k_{\min} = 50$\,keV by default). On a hit, the photon
energy $k$ is sampled by rejection from the Bethe--Heitler--Tsai differential
cross section (Section \ref{sec:physics-bremsformula}), the photon is pushed
onto the stack, and the parent kinetic energy is reduced by $k$.
\end{enumerate}

When the particle's kinetic energy falls below \texttt{Te\_cut} ($400$\,keV
by default), the residual $T$ is deposited locally and the track ends.
For positrons, an additional pair of 511\,keV $\gamma$s is generated
isotropically (back-to-back) at the termination point and pushed onto the
stack.

\subsubsection{Bremsstrahlung differential cross section}
\label{sec:physics-bremsformula}

The differential bremsstrahlung cross section in
\texttt{physics\_utils.jl::dsigma\_dk\_brems} is the Bethe--Heitler--Tsai
form with explicit Davies--Bethe--Maximon Coulomb correction $f_c(Z)$,
\begin{equation*}
f_c(Z) \;=\; (\alpha Z)^2 \!\left[\frac{1}{1 + (\alpha Z)^2} + 0.20206
- 0.0369\,(\alpha Z)^2 + 0.0083\,(\alpha Z)^4 - 0.002\,(\alpha Z)^6\right],
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d\sigma}{dk} \;=\; \frac{4\,\alpha\,r_e^2}{3\,k} \!\left[
\bigl(y^2 + 2(1 + (1 - y)^2)\bigr)\,\bigl(Z^2 (F_{\mathrm{el}} - f_c) + Z\,F_{\mathrm{inel}}\bigr)
+ \frac{(1 - y)(Z^2 + Z)}{3}\right],
\end{equation*}
with $y = k/E_{\mathrm{tot}}$, $E_{\mathrm{tot}} = T + m_e$,
$F_{\mathrm{el}} = \ln(184.15\,Z^{-1/3})$, and
$F_{\mathrm{inel}} = \ln(1194\,Z^{-2/3})$. At material load time, the total
cross section above $k_{\min}$ and a rejection-sampling ceiling $M(T) =
\max_{k}\bigl(k\,d\sigma/dk\bigr) \times 1.05$ are pre-tabulated on a
200-point logarithmic $T$ grid; both are stored on the \texttt{Material}
object as pre-logged arrays for fast log-log interpolation.

\subsection{Cross-section data: XCOM and ESTAR}
\label{sec:physics-tables}

All photon and electron tabulated data come from NIST:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[XCOM] photon mass attenuation coefficients [cm$^2$/g] on a per-material
energy grid, with seven channels: coherent, incoherent, photoelectric,
pair-nuclear, pair-electron, total-with-coherent, total-without-coherent.
LXeMC uses incoherent + photoelectric + (pair-nuclear $+$ pair-electron) =
$\sigma_C + \sigma_{\mathrm{Ph}} + \sigma_P$ for transport; the coherent
(Rayleigh) channel is loaded into the table but \emph{not} used in the active
transport (Section \ref{sec:limitations}). Per-atom cross sections are
obtained from per-mass values via $\sigma = (\mu/\rho)\,A_{\mathrm{eff}}/N_A$.

\item[ESTAR] electron mass collision and radiative stopping powers
[MeV\,cm$^2$/g] and CSDA range [g/cm$^2$]. The collisional $S_{\mathrm{col}}$
drives the lepton energy loss in \texttt{transport\_lepton!}.
$S_{\mathrm{rad}}$ and the CSDA range are loaded but not used in production
transport; the bremsstrahlung discrete sampling above provides radiative
energy loss with explicit secondaries.
\end{description}

The CSV loaders are in \texttt{nist\_data.jl}, the per-material storage and
log-log interpolation helpers are in \texttt{materials.jl}, and the
hot-path interpolator
(\texttt{interp\_loglog\_prelogged}) uses pre-computed log arrays so a single
$\log$ call on the query point covers all three channels in
\texttt{sigma\_three}.

\section{Geometry}
\label{sec:geometry}

The geometry layer is split into three responsibilities: a generic library of
geometric primitives, a canonical detector representation loaded from JSON,
and a compiled fast-kernel partition optimized for the hot path. The split
between source-side and tracking-side geometry, and the consistency
requirement that ties them together, is described at the end of this section.

\subsection{Geometric primitives}
\label{sec:geometry-core}

The file \texttt{geometry\_core.jl} provides the shared shape and wrapper
layer. It is detector-agnostic and is used by both the detector geometry
and the source pipeline:

\begin{itemize}
\item primitive solids: \texttt{Cyl}, \texttt{CylShell}, \texttt{Box},
\texttt{Disk}, \texttt{Cap}, \texttt{DomedContainer}, \texttt{CappedCylinder};
\item logical wrappers (solid + position $\pm$ orientation):
\texttt{LCyl}, \texttt{LCylShell}, \texttt{LBox}, \texttt{LDisk};
\item materialized wrappers (logical $+$ \texttt{Material}):
\texttt{PCyl}, \texttt{PCylShell}, \texttt{PBox}, \texttt{PDisk}, all
$<:$ \texttt{PhysicalVolume};
\item geometric operations: containment tests \texttt{is\_inside}, mass
computation, analytic ray entry/exit distances for cylinders, cylindrical
shells, and ellipsoidal disks (\texttt{distance\_to\_entry},
\texttt{distance\_to\_exit}).
\end{itemize}

These primitives are not specialized for the fast kernel or for the FV stack;
they are the lingua franca of all geometric tests in LXeMC.

\subsection{Canonical tracking detector}
\label{sec:geometry-tracking}

The tracking detector is loaded from \texttt{detector\_lz\_v3.json} into a
tree of \texttt{TrackingDetector} nodes by \texttt{load\_tracking\_detector}.
Every volume declares two markers:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{tag}] simulation policy --- one of \texttt{world},
\texttt{vacuum}, \texttt{passive\_lxe}, \texttt{tpc\_active}, \texttt{fv},
\texttt{skin}, \texttt{structural}, parsed into the integer enum
\texttt{RegionTag} (\texttt{TAG\_WORLD}, \texttt{TAG\_VACUUM}, \dots).
\item[\texttt{role}] descriptive metadata used to identify distinguished
regions; the only role that carries semantic weight in the code is
\texttt{tracking\_envelope}.
\end{description}

Per-region capabilities (\texttt{inFastKernel}, \texttt{isXe},
\texttt{sensitive}, \texttt{ecut\_keV}, \texttt{dz\_mm}, \texttt{fv\_target})
are derived from the tag via \texttt{\_capabilities\_for\_tag}, with optional
JSON-level overrides. Adding or removing a region is therefore a JSON-only
change; no Julia code references the volume name strings in classification or
compilation logic.

The current LZ tracking partition (\texttt{detector\_lz\_v3.json}) is shown
in Table \ref{tab:tracking-regions}.

\subsection{Meaning of the canonical regions}
\label{sec:geometry-regions}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{AirDome}, \texttt{AirCyl}] Vacuum regions above the gate
plane (the ellipsoidal dome and the cylindrical gap between the gate at
$z = 145.6$\,cm and the dome equator at $z = 148.5$\,cm). Filled with
\texttt{Vacuum} material so the fast kernel propagates gammas straight
through.
\item[\texttt{LXe\_dome}] Passive-LXe ellipsoidal cap forming the bottom
dome of the detector envelope ($z \in [-68.7, -41.33]$\,cm).
\item[\texttt{LXe\_below\_FC}] Passive-LXe disk below the field-cage
envelope ($z \in [-41.33, -13.75]$\,cm, $R \leq 82.1$\,cm).
\item[\texttt{LXe\_below\_cathode}] Passive-LXe column below the cathode
($z \in [-13.75, 0]$\,cm, $R \leq 72.8$\,cm). This is the bottom-PMT
entry path: photons emitted by the bottom-PMT array must traverse it before
entering \texttt{BottomActive}.
\item[\texttt{TopActive}] Active xenon cylinder between the gate
($z = 145.6$\,cm) and the top of the FV ($z = 96$\,cm).
\item[\texttt{BarrelActive}] Active xenon shell around the FV, spanning the
FV longitudinal range ($z \in [26, 96]$\,cm) at $R \in [39, 72.8]$\,cm.
\item[\texttt{BottomActive}] Active xenon cylinder between the cathode
($z = 0$) and the bottom of the FV ($z = 26$\,cm).
\item[\texttt{FV}] Fiducial volume: the inner cylinder at $R \leq 39$\,cm,
$z \in [26, 96]$\,cm. Target for event selection and the handoff to the full
transport stack.
\item[\texttt{Skin}] Active xenon skin outside the field-cage region
($R \in [74.224, 82.1]$\,cm). Deposits here feed the skin-veto logic at a
higher threshold (\texttt{veto\_skin}).
\item[\texttt{FC\_PTFE}] PTFE reflector walls of the field cage
($R \in [72.8, 73.947]$\,cm). Structural; geometric obstruction only.
\item[\texttt{FC\_rings}] Titanium ring part of the field cage
($R \in [73.947, 74.224]$\,cm). Structural; geometric obstruction only.
\end{description}

The three passive-LXe primitives tile the LXe volume not claimed by active
or structural regions. Together with the three \texttt{tpc\_active} regions,
the \texttt{fv}, the \texttt{skin}, the two \texttt{structural} volumes,
and the two vacuum regions, they form a strict partition of the
\texttt{LZ\_detector} envelope: every point inside belongs to exactly one
inner region.

\subsection{Fast-kernel geometry}
\label{sec:geometry-fastkernel}

The \texttt{FastKernelGeometry} is compiled once from the
\texttt{TrackingDetector} tree by \texttt{compile\_fastkernel\_geometry}. The
construction is fully data-driven:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Iterate over every volume with capability \texttt{inFastKernel = true}.
\item Compile each into a \texttt{FastKernelRegion} carrying precomputed
scalar geometry (\texttt{rmin}, \texttt{rmax}, \texttt{zmin}, \texttt{zmax},
cap parameters), the integer \texttt{RegionTag}, and the material reference.
\item Identify the unique region with role \texttt{tracking\_envelope} as
the envelope (early-exit test in \texttt{classify\_fastkernel}); the
compiler errors out if it is missing or duplicated. There is no fallback
region: the geometry is required to be a strict partition of the envelope
interior (verified by the \texttt{FastKernel geometry partition} testset
at every load), so every point inside the envelope is classified into
exactly one inner region by construction.
\end{enumerate}

The hot-path classifier \texttt{classify\_fastkernel(fk, pos)} tests the
envelope first (\texttt{nothing} on miss), then loops over all other regions
in JSON declaration order, and returns the first match. If no inner region
matches, it returns \texttt{nothing} --- a missing match indicates a partition
gap, which the transport loop treats as an escape. The interaction-policy
dispatcher
\texttt{select\_interaction\_fastkernel} compares integer
\texttt{RegionTag} values; no volume name appears in the classification or
compilation logic.

The fast-kernel boundary distance \texttt{distance\_to\_boundary\_fastkernel}
is analytic for the supported region shapes (cylinder, cylindrical shell,
ellipsoidal cap), which is what makes the kernel fast: no generic CSG ray
intersection.

\textbf{Note on name-based dispatch in the transport layer.} While the
geometry compiler and classifier are fully data-driven (no volume names in
\texttt{compile\_fastkernel\_geometry} or \texttt{classify\_fastkernel}),
the transport-level helpers in \texttt{tracking\_fast.jl} do reference
region names for veto and policy decisions:
\texttt{\_visible\_threshold\_MeV} checks \texttt{"TopActive"},
\texttt{"BarrelActive"}, \texttt{"BottomActive"} for the TPC veto and
\texttt{"Skin"} for the skin veto; \texttt{\_is\_passive\_region} checks
the three passive-LXe names and the two FC names. Similarly,
\texttt{\_classify\_escape\_volume} in \texttt{tracking.jl} uses region
names to classify FV-escape deposits as \texttt{:active} or
\texttt{:passive}. Adding a new tracked region to the JSON therefore
requires updating these helpers if the region should participate in veto
or escape-volume classification.

\subsection{Source vs. tracking geometry: the two-file model}
\label{sec:geometry-twofile}

The detector is described by \emph{two} JSON files:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{source\_geometry\_lz\_v1.json} --- source-side geometry. Hosts
the radioactive emission volumes used by the source pipeline (Section
\ref{sec:source-pipeline}): the OCV and ICV titanium shells, the MLI
insulation, the field-cage PTFE and rings, the PMT components.
\item \texttt{detector\_lz\_v3.json} --- tracking-side geometry. Hosts only
the volumes that the per-step transport actually needs to see: the detector
envelope, the LXe regions (passive, active, FV), the Skin veto, the
field-cage structures that obstruct in-flight $\gamma$s, the gas dome and
gas gap above the gate.
\end{itemize}

The split is by \emph{contents}, not by dimensions. Tracking geometry is a
\emph{subset} of source geometry, restricted to volumes the per-step
transport must consider. Volumes that appear in both files
(\texttt{FC\_PTFE}, \texttt{FC\_rings}, \texttt{Skin}) carry identical
geometric dimensions on the two sides; the test suite enforces this strictly.

\subsection{ICV consistency fix}
\label{sec:geometry-icvfix}

A geometric ambiguity in earlier versions of the source geometry was that
the inner radius of the inner cryostat barrel was not exactly equal to the
inner radii of its top and bottom domes, so the surface where the source
flux was sampled (the ICV inner surface) did not coincide with the tracking
detector envelope at $R = 82.1$\,cm. The Virtual Envelope used by the source
pipeline (Section \ref{sec:source-pipeline}) had to be extracted from
\texttt{FastKernelGeometry} to paper over this; tests of geometric mass
became fragile.

The fix, applied in the current geometry, enforces a single inner radius
$R_{\mathrm{inner}} = 82.1$\,cm for all three ICV surfaces (barrel, top
head, bottom head). The wall thicknesses of the dome heads are then adjusted
to preserve the original measured dome masses (top: 0.802\,cm wall, 107.7\,kg;
bottom: 1.192\,cm wall, 141.4\,kg; barrel: 0.9\,cm wall). On the tracking
side, the gas region above the gate plane was split into two volumes ---
\texttt{AirCyl} (cylindrical gas gap, $z \in [145.6, 148.5]$\,cm at
$R = 82.1$\,cm) and \texttt{AirDome} (ellipsoidal dome above the equator at
$z = 148.5$\,cm) --- so that the tracking detector envelope coincides
exactly with the ICV inner surface where the Virtual Envelope sits. The
Virtual Envelope is built directly from the source geometry (Section
\ref{sec:source-pipeline}).

\section{Full-stack transport}
\label{sec:fullstack}

The full-stack transport is the high-fidelity tier (Tier 1, Section
\ref{sec:tiers}). It is invoked only inside the FV after the fast kernel
hands off a $\gamma$ that has survived all veto cuts. The implementation is
in \texttt{tracking.jl} and is built around three functions and a stack
data structure.

\subsection{Stack model}
\label{sec:fullstack-stack}

A particle on the stack is a \texttt{Track}:
\begin{verbatim}
struct Track
    kind::Symbol           # :gamma, :electron, :positron
    energy::Float64        # MeV — kinetic for leptons, total for photons
    position::Vector{Float64}
    direction::Vector{Float64}  # unit
    track_id::Int          # unique
    parent_id::Int
    generation::Int        # cascade depth (0 = primary)
    interaction::Symbol    # process that created this track
    volume::Symbol         # volume where the track was created
end
\end{verbatim}
The \texttt{ParticleStack} is a LIFO stack (a struct wrapping
\texttt{Vector\{Track\}}); secondaries
created during a step are pushed and resumed by \texttt{propagate\_gamma}
when their parent's transport returns. A safety cap
\texttt{cfg.generation\_cap} (default $100$) prevents runaway cascades:
tracks with \texttt{generation > cap} are silently discarded when popped.

Energy deposits are recorded as
\texttt{Deposit(position, energy, source, interaction, volume)} with a
\texttt{source} symbol identifying the particle species, an
\texttt{interaction} symbol recording the process
(\texttt{:compton}, \texttt{:photoelectric}, \texttt{:collisional},
\texttt{:escaped\_gamma}, \texttt{:escaped\_lepton}, \texttt{:gamma\_local}),
and a \texttt{volume} symbol classifying the deposit location
(\texttt{:fv}, \texttt{:active}, \texttt{:passive}).
The deposit list is shared across the full cascade
of one primary $\gamma$ and is returned at the end as
\texttt{Vector\{Deposit\}}.

\subsection{\texttt{propagate\_gamma}: the cascade driver}
\label{sec:fullstack-driver}

\texttt{propagate\_gamma(E\_MeV, vol, fk, cfg; position, direction, rng)} is the
public entry point. The \texttt{fk} parameter (a \texttt{FastKernelGeometry})
is used to classify the volume of deposits that escape the FV boundary
(via \texttt{\_classify\_escape\_volume}). It pushes the primary $\gamma$
onto a fresh stack, then
loops:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item pop a track; skip if \texttt{generation > generation\_cap};
\item dispatch on \texttt{kind}: $\gamma$s go to \texttt{transport\_photon!},
leptons go to \texttt{transport\_lepton!};
\item the called function transports the particle until it exits, dies, or
falls below the cutoff, pushing any secondaries it produces onto the same
stack;
\item repeat until the stack is empty.
\end{enumerate}
The result is the full deposit list inside \texttt{vol}. Note that the
\texttt{vol} passed to \texttt{propagate\_gamma} is the FV
\texttt{PhysicalVolume} (a \texttt{PCyl} compiled from the FV node by
\texttt{compile\_fv\_volume}); transport stops at the FV boundary because
\texttt{is\_inside(vol, pos)} returns false. When a particle exits the
FV, the helper \texttt{\_classify\_escape\_volume(fk, pos)} classifies the
escape position as \texttt{:active} (Skin, TopActive, BarrelActive,
BottomActive), \texttt{:passive} (LXe\_dome, LXe\_below\_FC,
LXe\_below\_cathode, FC\_PTFE, FC\_rings, or outside the detector), or
\texttt{:fv}, and the deposit records the classified volume.

\subsection{\texttt{transport\_photon!}: photon stepping}
\label{sec:fullstack-photon}

The signature is \texttt{transport\_photon!(track, vol, fk, deposits,
stack, track\_counter, cfg, rng)}; the \texttt{fk} parameter is passed
through to \texttt{\_classify\_escape\_volume} for deposits that escape
the FV boundary.

The photon transport loop runs while $E \geq$ \texttt{Egamma\_cut} ($10$\,keV):

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Compute $(\sigma_C, \sigma_P, \sigma_{\mathrm{Ph}})$ at the current
energy via \texttt{sigma\_three(mat, E)}; sample interaction distance
$s \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}})$.
\item Advance: $\mathbf{x} \leftarrow \mathbf{x} + s\,\hat{\mathbf{d}}$. If the
new position is outside \texttt{vol}, return (the $\gamma$ has escaped the FV).
\item Choose process by branching ratios:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Compton.} Sample $E_\gamma'$, $\cos\theta$ from Klein--Nishina;
push the recoil electron of kinetic energy $T_e = E - E_\gamma'$ onto the
stack; rotate the $\gamma$ direction in the local frame by
$(\theta, \phi)$ and continue with $E \leftarrow E_\gamma'$.
\item \textbf{Pair.} Sample $\epsilon = E_{\mathrm{pos}}/E$; push positron
($T_{\mathrm{pos}} = \epsilon E - m_e$) and electron
($T_{\mathrm{ele}} = (1 - \epsilon) E - m_e$) onto the stack with sampled
polar angles; the parent $\gamma$ dies (\texttt{return}).
\item \textbf{Photoelectric.} Deposit $E_K$ locally as
\texttt{:photoelectric}; if $T_e = E - E_K > $ \texttt{Te\_cut}, push the
photoelectron with sampled angle; otherwise deposit $T_e$ locally; return.
If $E < E_K$, deposit the full $E$ locally.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

When the loop exits because $E < $ \texttt{Egamma\_cut}, the residual
energy is deposited locally as \texttt{:gamma\_local}.

\subsection{\texttt{transport\_lepton!}: condensed-history stepping}
\label{sec:fullstack-lepton}

The signature is \texttt{transport\_lepton!(track, vol, fk, deposits,
stack, track\_counter, cfg, rng)}, mirroring \texttt{transport\_photon!}.

The lepton transport loop runs while $T \geq $ \texttt{Te\_cut} ($400$\,keV):

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Compute $S_{\mathrm{col}}/\rho$ at the current $T$ via
\texttt{dEdx\_collision}; the linear stopping power is
$dE/dx = S_{\mathrm{col}}\,\rho$.
\item Choose step length \texttt{ds = ds\_step}; if the nominal energy loss
$\Delta E = (dE/dx)\,\Delta s$ would exceed $T$, shorten the step to
$0.9\,T/(dE/dx)$.
\item Deposit $\Delta E$ at the step midpoint with source
\texttt{:electron} or \texttt{:positron}.
\item Advance the position by $\Delta s$. If the new position is outside
\texttt{vol}, return.
\item If $T > k_{\min}$, sample discrete bremsstrahlung with per-step
probability $P_{\mathrm{brems}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{atom}}\,
\sigma_{\mathrm{brems}}(T)\,\Delta s,\ 0.5)$. On a hit, sample $k$ from
$d\sigma/dk$ by rejection (using the pre-tabulated ceiling $M(T)$), push the
photon with sampled angle, and reduce $T$ by $k$.
\end{enumerate}

When the loop exits ($T < $ \texttt{Te\_cut}), the residual $T$ is
deposited locally if the particle is still inside the volume. If the parent
\texttt{kind} was \texttt{:positron}, two 511\,keV $\gamma$s are pushed
back-to-back in a random direction at the termination position to model
in-flight or at-rest annihilation.

\subsection{Material lookup}
\label{sec:fullstack-materials}

\texttt{transport\_photon!} and \texttt{transport\_lepton!} read material
properties from \texttt{vol.material::Material}. For the FV, the material is
liquid xenon (active, with XCOM and ESTAR loaded and bremsstrahlung
pre-tabulated). The same transport routines could in principle be used for
any active \texttt{PhysicalVolume}, which is how the same code is reused for
calibration tests in passive volumes; in production they are called only
inside the FV.

\section{Fast-kernel transport}
\label{sec:fastkernel}

The fast kernel implements Tiers 2 and 3 (Section \ref{sec:tiers}) for
$\gamma$ transport outside the FV. It is in \texttt{tracking\_fast.jl} and
is the production entry point for every primary $\gamma$ before any FV
handoff.

\subsection{\texttt{transport\_gamma\_fastkernel}: the loop}
\label{sec:fastkernel-loop}

The function \texttt{transport\_gamma\_fastkernel(gamma, fk, cfg, rng;
max\_cm=400.0)} returns a \texttt{FastGammaTrackResult} with one of the
status values listed in Table \ref{tab:fastkernel-status}.

The main loop runs while $E \geq $ \texttt{Egamma\_cut} and the path length
remains below \texttt{max\_cm}:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Classify the current position: \texttt{region =
classify\_fastkernel(fk, pos)}. If \texttt{nothing}, the $\gamma$ has left the
detector envelope; return \texttt{:escaped}.
\item If the region is \texttt{FV}, return \texttt{:handoff\_fv} with the
current state (\texttt{event.jl} will hand the state to
\texttt{propagate\_gamma}).
\item Compute the analytic boundary distance $s_{\mathrm{bnd}}$ within the
current region. If the material is vacuum (no XCOM data, density $\leq 0$),
advance to the boundary and continue.
\item Sample interaction distance
$s_{\mathrm{int}} \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}})$. If
$s_{\mathrm{int}} > s_{\mathrm{bnd}}$ within tolerance, advance to the
boundary and continue (no interaction this step).
\item Otherwise advance to the interaction point, sample the process by
\texttt{(sC/s\_tot, sP/s\_tot, sPh/s\_tot)}, and apply region-dependent
policy (next subsection).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Interaction policy and veto checks}
\label{sec:fastkernel-policy}

The fast kernel does \emph{not} push secondaries; charged particles produced
in a step are taken to deposit locally (kerma approximation, justified by
the $<\!1$\,mm range of MeV electrons in dense detector materials). Each
process therefore makes a single energy-deposit decision:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[Compton.] Deposit the recoil energy $E - E_\gamma'$ locally in the
current region. Then check the veto threshold for that region:
$\geq$ \texttt{veto\_skin} in a Skin region returns
\texttt{:vetoed\_skin}; $\geq$ \texttt{veto\_TPC} in any
\texttt{TopActive}/\texttt{BarrelActive}/\texttt{BottomActive} region
returns \texttt{:vetoed\_tpc}. If neither veto fires, the $\gamma$ continues
with reduced energy $E_\gamma'$ along the rotated direction --- but first
the ROI floor is checked (next paragraph).
\item[Photoelectric / Pair.] Terminal: the full $\gamma$ energy is deposited
locally and the loop exits with the appropriate region-dependent status
(\texttt{:vetoed\_skin}, \texttt{:vetoed\_tpc},
\texttt{:absorbed\_passive}, or \texttt{:below\_cut}).
\end{description}

\subsection{ROI floor}
\label{sec:fastkernel-roifloor}

After a Compton scatter, if the surviving $\gamma$ energy is below
\texttt{E\_roi\_floor} (default $2.3$\,MeV), the $\gamma$ can no longer
contribute a deposit in the ROI window around $Q_{\beta\beta} = 2.458$\,MeV
under any reasonable resolution model. The fast kernel therefore collapses
the remaining energy onto the most recent deposit (replacing it with the
sum), and exits with the region-dependent terminal status. This is an
analysis-motivated approximation: it overestimates local energy deposition
slightly but is safe for background estimation because any history
terminated this way is far below the ROI window. The floor is configurable;
it should be lowered for analyses with wider ROI windows or with an
energy-resolution tail that extends below the default.

\subsection{FV handoff}
\label{sec:fastkernel-handoff}

The handoff is the only place where the fast kernel and the full stack
communicate. When \texttt{classify\_fastkernel} returns the FV region, the
fast kernel returns immediately with status \texttt{:handoff\_fv}, the
current $\gamma$ energy, position, and direction. The event loop
(\texttt{process\_event}) recognizes this status and calls
\texttt{propagate\_gamma} with the handed-off state, appending the resulting
deposits to the FV deposit list. Compton scatters that occurred in passive
or active regions outside the FV before the handoff produced their own local
deposits during fast-kernel transport; those deposits drive veto decisions
\emph{during} fast-kernel transport but are \emph{not} returned in
\texttt{EventProcessingResult.deposits} (Section \ref{sec:event}), because
they are not relevant to the topology classification of the FV event.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Status & Meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{:handoff\_fv}      & Reached the FV; hand off to \texttt{propagate\_gamma} \\
\texttt{:escaped}          & Left the detector envelope \\
\texttt{:below\_cut}       & Energy fell below \texttt{Egamma\_cut} \\
\texttt{:absorbed\_passive}& Absorbed in passive LXe or structural material \\
\texttt{:below\_roi\_fv}   & ROI-floor termination after a Compton scatter \\
\texttt{:vetoed\_tpc}      & Deposit in active TPC outside FV exceeded \texttt{veto\_TPC} \\
\texttt{:vetoed\_skin}     & Deposit in Skin exceeded \texttt{veto\_skin} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Status values returned by
\texttt{transport\_gamma\_fastkernel} (defined in
\texttt{tracking\_fast.jl::FastGammaTrackResult}).}
\label{tab:fastkernel-status}
\end{table}

\subsection{Diagnostic single-step variant}
\label{sec:fastkernel-diagnostic}

A single-interaction variant \texttt{propagate\_gamma\_fastkernel} lives in
\texttt{tracking\_util.jl}. It returns after the first interaction (Compton,
pair, or photoelectric) rather than continuing the multi-scatter loop. It is
used for geometry validation, transport debugging, and per-channel
diagnostic studies, and is \emph{not} part of the production event pipeline.
The same file holds \texttt{process\_event\_fastkernel\_calib}, a
calibration wrapper that samples a controlled $\gamma$ multiplicity from the
\texttt{"calib"} dispatch and packs the per-event multiplicity and primary
energies into a \texttt{FastKernelCalibEventResult} for reporting.

\section{Event processing}
\label{sec:event}

A physics event in LXeMC is a list of correlated primary $\gamma$s --- for
example, the four lines of a single Tl-208 decay (main 2.615\,MeV plus up to
three companions). Event-level processing is the orchestration of the fast
kernel and the FV stack across all $\gamma$s of one event, and the production
of the truth-level deposit list consumed by offline analysis. It lives in
\texttt{event.jl}.

\subsection{\texttt{process\_event}: the orchestrator}
\label{sec:event-process}

\texttt{process\_event(gammas, fk, vol, cfg, rng)} loops over the gammas of
one event:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item For each $\gamma$, call \texttt{transport\_gamma\_fastkernel}.
\item If the result is \texttt{:vetoed\_tpc} or \texttt{:vetoed\_skin},
short-circuit the entire event: set the corresponding boolean flag and
return immediately with status \texttt{:vetoed} and an empty deposit list.
\item If the result is \texttt{:handoff\_fv}, call
\texttt{propagate\_gamma(E, vol, fk, cfg; pos, dir, rng)} with the handed-off
state and append the returned deposits to the cumulative FV deposit list.
\item Any other status (\texttt{:escaped}, \texttt{:below\_cut},
\texttt{:absorbed\_passive}, \texttt{:below\_roi\_fv}) is treated as a
silent termination of that one $\gamma$; the loop continues with the next
$\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}

After the loop:
\begin{itemize}
\item if any FV deposits accumulated, return \texttt{:fv} with the full
deposit list;
\item otherwise return \texttt{:no\_fv} with an empty list.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texttt{EventProcessingResult}}
\label{sec:event-result}

The return value is
\begin{verbatim}
struct EventProcessingResult
    status::Symbol            # :fv, :vetoed, :no_fv
    has_tpc_veto::Bool
    has_skin_veto::Bool
    n_processed::Int          # gammas processed before short-circuit
    deposits::Vector{Deposit} # FV deposits for :fv events; empty otherwise
end
\end{verbatim}

The three status values have the following meanings:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{:fv}] At least one $\gamma$ reached the FV and produced
deposits there; \texttt{deposits} contains the full per-deposit truth list
for offline SS/MS classification.
\item[\texttt{:vetoed}] An anti-coincidence veto fired on some $\gamma$
during fast-kernel transport; the event is rejected. The booleans
\texttt{has\_tpc\_veto} and \texttt{has\_skin\_veto} indicate which veto
fired (one will be true at the moment of return; both can be relevant in
extensions). \texttt{n\_processed} is the index of the gamma that triggered
the veto.
\item[\texttt{:no\_fv}] No $\gamma$ reached the FV --- all were escaped,
absorbed, or terminated below the ROI floor. The event is not vetoed but
contributes no FV deposits.
\end{description}

\subsection{Deposit output}
\label{sec:event-output}

For \texttt{:fv} events, \texttt{deposits} is a flat list of
\texttt{Deposit} records with five fields: \texttt{position} (cm),
\texttt{energy} (MeV), \texttt{source} (particle species),
\texttt{interaction} (process), and \texttt{volume} (deposit location
classification). The \texttt{source} symbol identifies the particle:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{:electron}, \texttt{:positron} --- collisional energy loss
along a charged-track step;
\item \texttt{:photoelectric} --- the K-shell binding $E_K$ deposited
locally on photoelectric absorption (and any below-cut photoelectron
residual);
\item \texttt{:gamma}, \texttt{:gamma\_local} --- $\gamma$-originated
deposits (escaped photon or residual energy below \texttt{Egamma\_cut}).
\end{itemize}
The \texttt{interaction} symbol records the process (\texttt{:compton},
\texttt{:photoelectric}, \texttt{:collisional}, \texttt{:escaped\_gamma},
\texttt{:escaped\_lepton}, \texttt{:gamma\_local}). The \texttt{volume}
symbol classifies where the deposit occurred (\texttt{:fv},
\texttt{:active}, \texttt{:passive}), determined by
\texttt{\_classify\_escape\_volume} for deposits outside the FV boundary.

This list is the \emph{only} output of LXeMC for accepted events. It is
written to disk by the offline workflow (Section \ref{sec:source-pipeline})
and consumed by the FV analysis on the \texttt{offline\_fv\_analysis} branch
for clustering, energy-resolution smearing, ROI cuts, and SS/MS
classification.

\subsection{Offline clustering helpers}
\label{sec:event-clustering}

For convenience, \texttt{analysis.jl} provides two helpers used by both
in-process diagnostics and the offline pipeline:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{cluster\_deposits\_in\_z(deposits, dz\_cm; E\_min)} --- merge
deposits into clusters whose $z$-extent is within \texttt{dz\_cm}; clusters
below \texttt{E\_min} are discarded. Returns
\texttt{(z\_centroid, total\_energy)} tuples.
\item \texttt{is\_single\_site(deposits, dz\_cm; E\_min)} --- convenience
wrapper that returns \texttt{true} iff the deposit list forms at most one
cluster above \texttt{E\_min}.
\end{itemize}

The default values \texttt{cfg.dz\_resolution = 0.30}\,cm and
\texttt{cfg.E\_cluster\_min = 10}\,keV are placeholders; SS/MS classification
is expected to evolve, and any production analysis should set its own
values explicitly.

\section{Source flux pipeline}
\label{sec:source-pipeline}

The source flux pipeline computes the rate and spectral distribution of
$\gamma$s arriving at the detector from radioactive source volumes. It is
the implementation of Tier 2 (Section \ref{sec:tiers}) and is decoupled from
the detector-side transport: the expensive Monte Carlo step runs once per
source configuration, produces tabulated $(E, u)$ surface fluxes, and the
detector pipeline reuses those tables across many analysis variants.

\subsection{Source taxonomy}
\label{sec:source-taxonomy}

Sources are loaded from \texttt{source\_geometry\_lz\_v1.json} into
\texttt{SourceVolumeInfo} records by \texttt{load\_source\_geometry}. Each
source carries a geometry (a \texttt{PhysicalVolume}), a material, an
activity dictionary (mBq/kg per isotope), a mass (computed from geometry or
overridden via \texttt{equivalent\_mass\_kg}), a transport mode
(\texttt{:KN} or \texttt{:transparent}), and a class
(\texttt{shell\_source} or \texttt{virtual\_source}).

The currently supported sources (\texttt{supported\_sources()}) are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{cryostat\_barrel} --- compound: OCV barrel, MLI, ICV barrel.
\item \texttt{cryostat\_top} --- compound: OCV top head, ICV top head.
\item \texttt{cryostat\_bottom} --- compound: OCV bottom head, ICV bottom
head.
\item \texttt{pmt\_top} --- merged transparent disk over the three top PMT
sub-components (PMTs, bases, structure).
\item \texttt{pmt\_bottom} --- merged transparent disk over the four bottom
PMT sub-components (PMTs, bases, structure, R8778 dome).
\item \texttt{pmt\_bottom\_lxe} --- same bottom PMT components as
\texttt{pmt\_bottom}, with compound propagation through the passive LXe
slab between the PMT array and the cathode; flux recorded at the cathode
boundary.
\item \texttt{pmt\_barrel} --- merged transparent cylindrical shell over the
three barrel PMT sub-components (cables, R8520 skin PMTs, R8778 lower-ring
PMTs) at $R \approx 81.2$--$81.4$\,cm, with inward radial filtering.
\end{itemize}

Supported isotopes (\texttt{supported\_isotopes()}) are \texttt{Bi214} and
\texttt{Tl208}, the two dominant late-chain $\gamma$ emitters in the
$0\nu\beta\beta$ ROI window. Extending to a new (source, isotope) pair
requires adding a dispatch entry and (for non-trivial geometries) a flux
function.

\subsection{Flux tables}
\label{sec:source-fluxtables}

The central data product of the source pipeline is a two-dimensional
probability table in $(E, u)$ where $E$ is the $\gamma$ energy at the exit
of the source material and $u = \cos\theta$ is the angle between the
$\gamma$ direction and the inward surface normal (toward the LXe). Per
generated decay,
\begin{equation*}
\texttt{pdf}[i,j] \;=\; \frac{N_{\mathrm{surviving\ in\ bin\ }(i,j)}}{N_{\mathrm{generated}}}.
\end{equation*}
Absolute rates are obtained downstream by multiplying by activity $\times$
mass $\times$ branching ratio.

Two concrete flux types live in \texttt{source\_flux.jl}:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{SourceFluxBi214}] One table for the single $2.448$\,MeV
$\gamma$ per $^{214}$Bi decay. The chain-to-line branching is
\texttt{BR\_BI214\_2448 = 0.015}.
\item[\texttt{SourceFluxTl208}] One table for the main $2.615$\,MeV $\gamma$
plus one table per companion line ($583$\,keV at 85\%, $511$\,keV at 23\%,
$861$\,keV at 12\%). At sampling time the main line always fires; each
companion fires as an independent Bernoulli trial with its branching ratio,
producing 1--4 $\gamma$s per decay. The chain-to-main-line branching is
\texttt{BR\_TL208\_2615 = 0.3594 $\times$ 0.998 $\approx$ 0.3587} (the
$^{212}$Bi $\to$ $^{208}$Tl branch times the $^{208}$Tl $\to$ 2.615\,MeV
branch).
\end{description}

A \texttt{SourceRateTable} carries the activity-weighted sum of component
flux tables for one ICV surface, in units of $\gamma$s/sec per $(E, u)$
bin: $\mathrm{pdf\_rate}[i,j] = \sum_k A_k\,m_k\,\mathrm{BR}\,\mathrm{pdf}_k[i,j]$.

\subsection{Compound propagation}
\label{sec:source-compound}

$\gamma$s emitted from the outer cryostat must traverse the vacuum gap and
the inner cryostat before entering the LXe. This is handled by
\texttt{propagate\_through\_layers(E, pos, dir, layers, cfg, rng)}: the
$\gamma$ flies in a straight line to the entry of the next layer's logical
volume (analytic \texttt{distance\_to\_entry} for cylinders, shells, and
ellipsoidal disks), then \texttt{propagate\_in\_source} runs Klein--Nishina
photon-only transport inside the layer until exit or absorption. The status
return is \texttt{:exited}, \texttt{:absorbed}, or \texttt{:lost} (if the
ray misses a layer).

The source-side photon transport in \texttt{propagate\_in\_source} is the
photon part of the full stack: same XCOM cross sections, same Klein--Nishina
sampler, same energy cutoffs --- but no electron stack and no bremsstrahlung,
which is the kerma approximation appropriate to dense source materials at
MeV energies.

\subsection{Cryostat sources}
\label{sec:source-cryostat}

For the LZ cryostat, each surface (barrel, top head, bottom head) sums over
the contributing source layers:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[Barrel] Three components: OCV barrel $\to$ ICV barrel (compound; MLI
is vacuum and is correctly skipped from the layer chain because the $\gamma$
flies straight through it), MLI $\to$ ICV barrel (compound, with MLI as a
transparent source), and ICV barrel self-emission.
\item[Top head] Two components: OCV top $\to$ vacuum $\to$ ICV top
(compound), and ICV top self-emission.
\item[Bottom head] Two components: OCV bottom $\to$ vacuum $\to$ ICV bottom
(compound), and ICV bottom self-emission.
\end{description}

Each component produces its own \texttt{SourceFluxBi214} and
\texttt{SourceFluxTl208}; an activity-weighted sum (\texttt{\_build\_rate\_table})
gives the combined rate table per surface. Cross-contributions
(e.g., OCV barrel $\to$ ICV top) are neglected because their solid-angle
acceptance is small compared to the same-surface contributions.

\subsection{PMT sources}
\label{sec:source-pmt}

The PMT arrays are bundled groups of small components (PMTs, bases,
structure, R8778 dome on the bottom). Because they are transparent sources
and share a common geometric configuration, the per-component flux tables
are essentially identical (a $\sim 50\%$ hemisphere acceptance, no material
attenuation). The pipeline lumps each group into a single merged volume,
generates one shared flux table, and recovers per-component rates by
mass$\,\times\,$activity bookkeeping in the final rate table.

Four PMT flux functions are implemented:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{pmt\_top\_flux}] Three sub-components merged into a flat
\texttt{PDisk} with \texttt{:up} orientation (downward toward LXe).
\item[\texttt{pmt\_bottom\_flux}] Four sub-components merged into a flat
\texttt{PDisk} with \texttt{:down} orientation (upward toward LXe).
\item[\texttt{pmt\_bottom\_lxe\_flux}] Same bottom PMT components, but with
compound propagation through the passive LXe slab from the PMT array
to the cathode. The flux is recorded at the cathode boundary, giving
the spectrum after attenuation in the passive LXe column.
\item[\texttt{pmt\_barrel\_flux}] Three barrel sub-components (cables,
R8520 skin PMTs, R8778 lower-ring PMTs) merged into a transparent
\texttt{PCylShell} with inward radial filtering.
\end{description}

\subsection{Virtual envelope}
\label{sec:source-ve}

$\gamma$s sampled from the flux tables must be placed on a physical surface
with a concrete 3D position and direction before being injected into the
fast kernel. This surface is the \emph{Virtual Envelope} (VE). It is built
directly from the source geometry by
\texttt{make\_virtual\_envelope(source, sg)}; thanks to the ICV consistency
fix (Section \ref{sec:geometry-icvfix}), the cryostat VE sits exactly on the
ICV inner surface at $R = 82.1$\,cm.

The supported envelope kinds are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrl}
\toprule
Surface & \texttt{kind} & $R$ [cm] & Source geometry \\
\midrule
Cryostat barrel & \texttt{:barrel}    & $82.09$ & ICV\_barrel inner cylinder \\
Cryostat top    & \texttt{:cap\_up}   & $82.09$ & ICV\_top inner ellipsoidal cap \\
Cryostat bottom & \texttt{:cap\_down} & $82.09$ & ICV\_bottom inner ellipsoidal cap \\
PMT top         & \texttt{:disk\_flat}& $72.8$  & merged top PMT disk \\
PMT bottom      & \texttt{:disk\_flat}& $72.8$  & merged bottom PMT disk \\
PMT bottom (LXe)& \texttt{:disk\_flat}& $72.8$  & cathode exit disk \\
PMT barrel      & \texttt{:barrel}    & $\sim 81.2$ & merged barrel PMT shell \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A small inset \texttt{ENVELOPE\_INSET\_CM = 0.01}\,cm is applied to all VEs
(hence $R = 82.09$ instead of $82.1$\,cm) to avoid floating-point boundary
ambiguity in the fast-kernel region classifier.

\subsection{Remaining sources: field cage}
\label{sec:source-remaining}

Six field-cage sources are defined in the source geometry but not yet
implemented in the flux pipeline:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5cm,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{FC\_PTFE}, \texttt{FC\_rings}] Dense sources (PTFE, Ti) with
KN self-shielding. Emit directly into the LXe; single-source flux generation
(no compound propagation). Barrel-type VE.
\item[\texttt{FC\_topgrid}, \texttt{FC\_botgrid}] Stainless-steel grid
holders (thick annular slabs). Dense, KN transport.
\item[\texttt{FC\_resistors}, \texttt{FC\_sensors}] Transparent sources
(mass-equivalent carriers on the field cage). Same pattern as the PMT
transparent sources.
\end{description}

Each will require a dispatch entry and a flux function; the infrastructure
(\texttt{dispatch\_source\_flux}, \texttt{make\_virtual\_envelope},
flux table I/O) is already in place.

\subsection{Three-stage offline workflow}
\label{sec:source-stages}

The end-to-end background computation is split into three scripts:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Flux generation} (\texttt{generate\_flux\_tables.jl}). For one
(source, isotope) pair, generate $N$ decays, propagate through the source
material (and any compound layers), and write the $(E, u)$ flux tables as
CSV files plus a \texttt{metadata.json}. This is the expensive Monte Carlo
step; it parallelizes naturally over Julia threads (\texttt{julia -t N}).
Run once per source configuration.
\item \textbf{Background preselection} (\texttt{run\_source\_backgrounds.jl}).
Load the flux tables, sample $\gamma$s onto the Virtual Envelope, run each
event through \texttt{process\_event}, and write the surviving events
(status \texttt{:fv}) to \texttt{candidates.csv} as
$(x, y, z, E_{\mathrm{deposited}})$ rows. Per-source rejection statistics
(veto fractions, no-FV fractions) go to \texttt{statistics.csv}. Also
parallelized.
\item \textbf{Final analysis} (\texttt{compute\_backgrounds.py}). Apply
Gaussian energy-resolution smearing and an ROI window cut to the candidate
list, compute absolute background rates in counts/year, and produce summary
plots and a background table.
\end{enumerate}

The flux tables are the natural reuse boundary: a change to the energy
resolution or the ROI window only requires re-running stage 3, and a change
to the analysis cuts only requires re-running stages 2 and 3.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

LXeMC is purposefully specialized: a fast staged simulation for an LZ-like
LXe TPC, accurate enough in the FV for $0\nu\beta\beta$ studies. The
following are known approximations and limitations; each is acceptable for
the current use case but would be a starting point for future work.

\paragraph{Photon physics.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No coherent (Rayleigh) scattering in the active transport.}
The XCOM coherent channel is loaded but \texttt{sigma\_three} returns only
incoherent + photoelectric + pair. Coherent scattering matters at
sub-100\,keV in heavy materials and at small angles; for $\gamma$s with
energies of interest near $Q_{\beta\beta}$ it is a $\sim$1\% effect on the
total cross section and a small angular bias. For a GXe configuration with a
broader low-energy reach this should be revisited.
\item \emph{No atomic relaxation cascade.} On photoelectric absorption the
K-shell binding is deposited locally as a single \texttt{:photoelectric}
deposit; fluorescent X-rays and Auger electrons that would carry that energy
on $\sim$mm scales are not generated explicitly. For SS/MS classification
in the LXe FV the spatial scale of the unmodelled cascade is below
\texttt{dz\_resolution} and the approximation is safe; in GXe it would
not be.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Lepton physics.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No multiple Coulomb scattering.} Charged-particle steps are
straight lines between collisional energy losses. In LXe at MeV energies
the CSDA range is sub-mm and the directional information of the secondary
electron is rapidly lost in any case; in GXe the centimetre-scale electron
tracks make multiple scattering essential and \texttt{transport\_lepton!}
would need a Highland-style step or full MS sampling.
\item \emph{No $\delta$-rays.} Hard knock-on electrons above a production
threshold are not produced as explicit secondaries; their energy is folded
into the continuous collisional loss via $S_{\mathrm{col}}$. Acceptable in
LXe, less so in GXe.
\item \emph{Positron annihilation only at end-of-track.} In-flight
annihilation is approximated by emitting two $m_e$ $\gamma$s back-to-back at
the termination position once the positron's kinetic energy falls below
\texttt{Te\_cut}. Genuine in-flight annihilation kinematics (boosted
back-to-back pair) is not sampled.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Fast kernel.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Photon-only transport with secondaries deposited locally.}
Charged secondaries from Compton, pair, or photoelectric in regions outside
the FV are taken to deposit at the interaction point. Justified by sub-mm
electron range in dense materials, but it does mean the spatial pattern of
energy deposition outside the FV is coarse-grained at the Compton-scatter
scale.
\item \emph{ROI-floor termination.} Histories whose $\gamma$ energy falls
below \texttt{E\_roi\_floor} after a Compton scatter are terminated locally
with the residual energy collapsed onto the last deposit. This is an
analysis-motivated approximation and slightly overestimates local deposits;
it is safe for background estimation in the ROI window but should be
disabled for any analysis whose region of interest extends below the floor.
\item \emph{Coaxial cylindrical geometry assumption.} The fast-kernel region
predicates assume all volumes are coaxial cylinders, shells, or ellipsoidal
caps centered on the detector axis. A non-coaxial geometry would require
extending \texttt{\_is\_inside\_fastkernel\_region} and
\texttt{distance\_to\_boundary\_fastkernel}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Geometry.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The ICV is modelled as a perfect cylinder $+$ two ellipsoidal domes,
with dome wall thicknesses adjusted to preserve the measured masses
(Section \ref{sec:geometry-icvfix}). The real ICV is not a perfect dome;
the simplification is deliberate to keep the source/tracking inner-surface
match exact at $R = 82.1$\,cm.
\item The \texttt{RegionTag} enum is closed: adding a genuinely new kind of
sensitive region requires extending the enum and
\texttt{\_capabilities\_for\_tag}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis layer.}
\begin{itemize}
\item SS/MS classification is currently a one-dimensional $z$-clustering
with a fixed scale (\texttt{dz\_resolution}). A real classifier will need
to combine $z$ and ($r$ or $xy$) clustering and probably an explicit
detector-response stage. The deposit-list output (Section
\ref{sec:event-output}) is designed to make this evolution easy without
touching the transport code.
\item Energy-resolution smearing and ROI cuts live in the Python stage
(\texttt{compute\_backgrounds.py}). The Julia transport produces only
truth-level energy.
\end{itemize}

These limitations are acceptable because they follow directly from the
project goal: simulate extremely large event samples quickly while
retaining the physics that drives the FV-side classification. They are
not architectural blockers --- each can be lifted independently.
